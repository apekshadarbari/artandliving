The Artist

Meet Apeksha

My first creative language was writing

In a land far, far away, over a quarter of a century ago, a little girl was not just discovering her love for writing but finding a channel, a safe space to say what she couldn't speak. She'd scribble little poems and musings at the back of all her notebooks and diaries, which she had many. She started journaling when she was eight. Odd she always thought, considering no one in her family did.

But she knew she was odd. She was sure of it by now.

She was also an avid reader, always hungry to read more (bookstores are still like Disneyland to me! And stationery stores! And art supplies stores! I digress). It was how she travelled many lands and lived many lives.

Once she left home for her engineering degree, at age 17, her writing practice was never the same again. Neither was her reading. The girl who never not had a book in her hand went months without reading a page. For the next two decades, there were hardly any environments that nurtured creativity and she kept becoming smaller and smaller to fit in those rooms.

But she never stopped scribbling her poems and ramblings. The backs of her now many, many, many notebooks and diaries still look much the same. But she also writes in the front now :)

Writing poetry was the first art form she ever practiced and now she's determined to not just write more, once again, but go after every form of art that calls out to her. She is an artist after all.

Why I do what I do

I was diagnosed with ADHD a couple of years ago. It felt like I didn't know I needed glasses until 35 and now I am seeing everything for the first time for what it actually looks like undistorted. I saw why I kept hitting a wall every few years, why everything felt so hard, how tightly wound I was constantly contorting myself to fit in boxes handed to me by others. How I was conditioned to always trust outside of myself over my instinct because my natural instinct is "naturally wrong."

Getting the diagnosis when I was already in deep burnout, made me reflect on the life I had built which looked splendid on paper but was hollowing me out from the inside. I started the slow, uncomfortable work of challenging my conditioning and reconnecting with my body. I realized I had spent most of my life tuning her out until she had to scream, constantly overriding my instinct because it was inconvenient, or too much, or didn't translate well into the language the room was speaking.

So I started listening again. And when I did, I got the itch to paint.

When I painted for the first time only a few years ago and created my first abstract piece, something inside me unlocked, something familiar and new at the same time. The same feeling I had known as a child with writing, a simultaneous pouring out and filling up. A physical release and a deep-seated satisfaction.

A portal had opened inside me and I haven't stopped since.

Painting became a reclamation of instinct, of trust, of a self I had spent a lifetime managing into smallness. Breaking the rules of what good art should be, of productivity and goals, and choosing to let my body speak what it wants to say.

Alongside those discoveries and inventions, I started to understand something larger. Creativity is not a talent some people have and others don't. It is a human need. It has been a need since our ancestors painted on cave walls. It is how we tell the story about our individual and universal human experience.

The particular kind of relief I felt when I painted, the permission of being in a process with no right answer, the state of being, not becoming. I started noticing that is where the magic happens.

And it is available to each one of us.

I completed my Therapeutic Arts Practitioner Certification because I want other women running on fumes to experience what I experienced, a homecoming through creating art. I got trained to hold this space ethically and in an informed way. But my biggest qualification is my journey on this path and what makes me the best person to hold this space for other women. I'm still on it and here as a companion and guide.

That is what Art & Living is. A philosophy to live in your truest expression, a safe room to practice choosing process over product, and a community which is the village every woman needs.

For the woman who is constantly holding everything, afraid that something will fall if she's not holding it all — you were not meant to run on fumes. Come, sit with me and let's tend to your cup.
